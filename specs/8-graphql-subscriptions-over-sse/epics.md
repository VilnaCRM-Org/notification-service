# Epics and Stories: GraphQL subscriptions over Server-Sent Events (issue #8)

This file cuts `architecture.md` of this bundle into stories. It adds no component. Every file, type, constant, limit, message, status code and problem text below is the one of `architecture.md` and `prd.md`. Where a story names a test list of the section "Test Strategy" of `architecture.md`, that list binds the story. A decision taken while cutting the stories is a line `> Assumption:` in the story it affects, or below when it affects the whole file.

Notation: `FR-<n>` and `NFR-<n>` are the requirements of this bundle's `prd.md`; `4/FR-<n>` are those of bundle 4; `P<n>` are the protocol choices of the PRD; `AC1` to `AC11` are the acceptance criteria of issue #8 in the order the issue lists them on 2026-11-17 (AC9 and AC10, the OpenAPI document and its gates, come from the maintainer's decision of 2026-11-17; the former last criterion is AC11).

**How the stories run:**

- The stories run in waves. A story is ready when the stories of its "Depends on" line are done, and the ready stories form one wave. One implementer works on each story of a wave, all in one working tree. Every story except 5.1 is implemented by the rust-implementer agent; story 5.1 is the serialized verify step of the plugin.
- A story that shares its wave with other stories runs only the five DB-free targets of `## Constraints` part (b): `make.format`, `make.lint_code`, `make.unit_tests`, `make.architecture` and `make.complexity`. A story that is alone in its wave may run every target it names.
- After each wave, one serialized verify step runs the targets that need the shared Kafka broker or the stack: `make.integration_tests`, `make.tests_with_coverage`, `make.e2e_tests` and `make.smoke_load_tests`, and `make.schemathesis_validate` for a wave whose story names it. It also runs the whole-tree gates that the stories of the wave name: `make.static_analysis`, `make.unused_dependencies`, the contract and documentation targets, `make.ci_cli` and `make.mutation_tests_diff`. `make.setup_test_db` and `make.sqlx_prepare` read `not available: capability absent` and are skipped and reported.
- Each story is committed on its own, through the hooks, as `<type>(#8): story N.M <title>`, with the type of the table "Waves".
- (i) Only the tree at the end of a wave has to compile and pass the lint and unused-dependency gates. A compile error or a finding in a file of another story of the same wave is not a story's failure.
- (ii) `Cargo.lock` is regenerated by cargo and never edited by hand, and a lock refresh never raises another crate's version (`make update` is not used). Only story 1.4 changes the dependency set (`humantime` out of the two service crates, `jiff` into `crates/service`), so no other story is expected to change the lock. Every make target that compiles runs cargo with `--locked`, so story 1.4 refreshes the lock with one raw call, the rust-implementer's allowed dependency probe `cargo tree -i jiff` without `--locked`, as its first step right after its manifest edits; it resolves the changed manifests and writes only the minimal lock change (`jiff`, `jiff-core`, `jiff-static` and `portable-atomic-util` added, no locked version raised). Until then the `--locked` targets of the other stories of wave 6 fail in the shared tree (rule i).
- (iii) Two stories of one wave never edit the same file, so no story rewrites a function, struct, test or section that another story of its wave writes. The "Scope" list of a story names the files and lines it owns. A file that stories of different waves edit is ordered by their "Depends on" edges, which therefore name every earlier story that edits a file of the story.
- (iv) A story uses only items that exist before its wave starts. A crate-private item is added by the story of its first non-test caller, because `unused` (dead code) and `unreachable_pub` are lints of `[workspace.lints]` and an item without a production caller fails `make.lint_code`.
- Every story carries its own tests to the bar of the repository:
  - 100 % line and function coverage of its code;
  - both sides of every boundary, every error branch and every log entry asserted;
  - every awaited call of a test inside `tokio::time::timeout`;
  - paused tokio time wherever a timer is involved, and a scripted fake that pends after its script;
  - wire blocks compared byte for byte, and result JSON compared with `serde_json`.
- No story plans an `#[allow]`, a `#[mutants::skip]`, a `#[coverage(off)]`, a coverage or mutation exclusion, an allow-list edit, a lowered bar or a protected-file edit. An `#[expect(.., reason = "..")]` is used only where `architecture.md` names it: a lint that the `MergedSubscription` expansion triggers in `crates/service/src/graphql/schema.rs`.
- The mutation run of a wave runs after the commits of the wave: `make.mutation_tests_diff` with `base_ref=<commit before the wave>`. A wave that changed production Rust and whose run names no mutant is not recorded as passed. A surviving or timed-out mutant is fixed by a further commit in the files of the owning story before the next wave.
- The story that changes the schema regenerates `.github/graphql-spec/spec` with `make.generate_graphql_spec` in the same commit, and each story that changes the OpenAPI document (4.4, 4.5) regenerates `.github/openapi-spec/spec.yaml` with `make.generate_openapi_spec` in its commit. No story adds a configuration variable or a log message.
- The story that first edits a file the template sync would restore adds it to `.templatesyncignore`. These qualify: `crates/service/src/graphql.rs` and `crates/service/src/graphql/handler.rs`, first edited by story 2.3, and `crates/service/src/http/ping.rs`, `crates/service/src/http/ping/`, `crates/service/src/http/docs.rs` and `crates/service/src/http/docs/`, first edited by story 4.5. Every other touched file is service-owned or already listed.
- A story that adds a request form adds its runnable example to `docs/requests.http`. The descriptive documents of FR-25 are written in Epic 4.
- No story presents a latency, throughput or capacity number as measured. The design target of bundle 4 stays a design target.

**Protected changes.** No story changes a file of `protected-config.toml`, the `[workspace.lints]` table or `.claude/rust-sdlc.yml`, and only story 1.4 changes `[workspace.dependencies]` (`humantime` out, `jiff` in, `prd.md` NFR-21). The maintainer approved three protected changes during implementation, each committed on its own outside the stories under `approved-config-change` and listed in "Protected Changes" of `architecture.md`: the `operation-success-response` override for the WebSocket operation in `.spectral.yaml` (`a374895`), the exclusion of that operation from Schemathesis in `make/gates.mk` (`71aab46`), and the two advisory entries RUSTSEC-2026-0542 and RUSTSEC-2026-0543 in `deny.toml` (`c4f83b3`, decision M4 of 2026-11-20). Pull request #9 already carries the finished cleanup of issue #7 (`07c2ff7` to `c36f708`). That cleanup holds an approved one-line deletion in each of the protected files `.prettierignore`, `_typos.toml` and `taplo.toml`, plus a profile refresh, all after the merge base `c4900bf`. Those commits are not part of this plan, and no story touches those four files. Apart from the approved exclusion, no story excludes an operation from Schemathesis or relaxes a check, and a failure that cannot be fixed at its root stops story 4.5 with an escalation to the maintainer.

### Story 1.1: One cursor rule with two answers

No protected change. Implemented by: the rust-implementer agent.

Goal: the carrier rule of the cursor (every carrier that is present must hold a cursor; when both do, the `Last-Event-ID` header wins over the `lastEventId` parameter) lives in one crate-internal function that reports which carrier failed. Story 1.3 can then answer that fault with `VALIDATION_FAILED`, while the REST routes keep their 400. It is a refactor that keeps every behaviour: no answer of the SSE or WebSocket route changes.

**Scope:**

- `crates/notification/event-stream/application/src/http/dto.rs`:
  - `CursorFault { carrier: &'static str }` (`Debug, Clone, Copy, PartialEq, Eq`) with `message` and `bad_request`;
  - `read_cursor(header: Option<&HeaderValue>, parameter: Option<&str>) -> Result<Option<Cursor>, CursorFault>`, into which today's private one-carrier helper is folded;
  - `parse_cursor` keeps its signature and its answers and becomes `read_cursor(headers.get(CURSOR_HEADER), parameter)` mapped with `CursorFault::bad_request`;
  - `types_problem` becomes `pub(crate)`.
- `crates/notification/event-stream/application/src/http/dto/tests.rs`.
- Not here: `CursorFault::violation`, which story 1.3, its first caller, adds.

**Covers:** FR-11 (one carrier rule, the header wins), FR-13 (the fault names its carrier), FR-23 (its tests), NFR-17 (REST answers unchanged).

**Tests:** unit tests of `read_cursor` and `CursorFault::message`. The existing tests of `parse_cursor`, of the SSE route and of the WebSocket route pass unchanged.

**Acceptance Criteria:**

- **Given** only a header, only a parameter, or neither, **When** `read_cursor` runs with the values `0`, `1` and `18446744073709551615`, **Then** it answers that cursor from the carrier given, and `None` when neither is present
- **Given** a valid header and a malformed parameter (`abc`), **When** `read_cursor` runs, **Then** it answers the `lastEventId` fault (a malformed parameter is refused also beside a valid header); **given** both valid, **Then** the header's cursor wins
- **Given** a header holding `abc`, `-1`, `+1`, ` 1`, an empty value, `18446744073709551616` or a value that is not visible ASCII text, **When** `read_cursor` runs, **Then** it answers `CursorFault` with the carrier `Last-Event-ID`, whatever the parameter holds
- **Given** no header and a parameter holding one of those faulty values, **When** `read_cursor` runs, **Then** it answers `CursorFault` with the carrier `lastEventId`
- **Given** each carrier, **When** `CursorFault::message` runs, **Then** it answers `Last-Event-ID must be a decimal unsigned 64-bit integer` or `lastEventId must be a decimal unsigned 64-bit integer`, and never holds the value
- **Given** the SSE and WebSocket routes, **When** their existing tests run, **Then** every cursor answer is unchanged: the 400 problem with the same `detail`, and the header winning over the parameter
- **Given** the story is complete, **When** the gates run, **Then** `make.format`, `make.lint_code`, `make.unit_tests`, `make.architecture` and `make.complexity` pass in the wave, and `make.static_analysis`, `make.tests_with_coverage` and `make.mutation_tests_diff` pass in the serialized verify step

**Depends on:** none

### Story 1.2: Event object built from the shared frame

No protected change. Implemented by: the rust-implementer agent.

Goal: the GraphQL object `Event` (`EventObject`) is built from the frame that the hub already shares, in the subscriber's task. Its `data` equals, member for member, the `data` line of the SSE route and the `data` member of the WebSocket frame for the same event. `Frame` gains no field, and the publish path does exactly what it does today.

**Scope:**

- New `crates/notification/event-stream/application/src/graphql/subscription.rs`:
  - `EventObject` (`Debug, Clone, PartialEq, Eq, SimpleObject`, `#[graphql(name = "Event")]`) with `id: String`, `event_type: String` (GraphQL name `type`) and `data: Json<async_graphql::Value>`;
  - `data` carries `#[graphql(complexity = 501)]`, with a plain `//` comment that gives the reason (more than half of the complexity limit 1 000, so a document selects it at most once); a `///` line would change the description;
  - the type and field documentation comments are the one-line descriptions of FR-1;
  - `impl From<&Frame> for EventObject`: `id` from `frame.sequence`, `type` from `frame.event_type`, and `data` from the member `data` of `frame.json`, read through a private struct `{ data: async_graphql::Value }`. A text that cannot be read gives `Value::Null` in the same expression, and the code documents this.
- New `crates/notification/event-stream/application/src/graphql/subscription/tests.rs`.
- `crates/notification/event-stream/application/src/graphql.rs`: `mod subscription;` and `pub use subscription::EventObject;`.

**Covers:** FR-1 (the `Event` object and the complexity of `data`), FR-10 (the object's content), FR-15 (the complexity limit), FR-23 (its tests), NFR-1 (no `Frame` field, parsing per subscriber), NFR-3 and NFR-8 (one copy of the event object per result).

**Tests:** unit tests of `EventObject::from(&Frame)` for frames written by `Frame::encode` and for a frame built by hand in the test; a test schema whose query root returns one `Event`, with `limit_complexity(1_000)`.

**Acceptance Criteria:**

- **Given** a frame encoded by `Frame::encode` for the event at offset 1042 of type `user.created`, **When** it is converted, **Then** `id` is `"1043"`, `type` is `"user.created"`, and `data` serialised as compact JSON equals the event object of the frame byte for byte
- **Given** an event object whose members are not in sorted order (`{"z":1,"a":2}` inside `data`), **When** it is converted and serialised, **Then** the producer's member order is kept, whatever features of `serde_json` the build unifies
- **Given** an event object whose strings hold line breaks, quotes, backslashes and characters outside the Basic Multilingual Plane, **When** it is converted and serialised, **Then** the value equals the frame's event object after a `serde_json` round trip
- **Given** a frame built by hand whose `json` cannot be read, **When** it is converted, **Then** `data` is `null` and nothing panics
- **Given** the object type, **When** it is registered in a test schema and printed as SDL, **Then** the type is `Event` with the fields `data: JSON!`, `id: String!` and `type: String!`, each with its description of FR-1, and no description holds `node`, `Connection`, `update`, `delete` or `input `
- **Given** a test schema whose query root field `event` returns one `Event` and whose complexity limit is 1 000, **When** `{ event { id type data } }`, `{ event { a: data b: data } }`, `{ event { data data } }` and `{ event { data ...F } } fragment F on Event { data }` are executed, **Then** the first runs, and each of the other three is refused with async-graphql's complexity error before any field is resolved
- **Given** `Frame` and `Hub::deliver`, **When** the story's diff is read, **Then** neither changed
- **Given** the story is complete, **When** the gates run, **Then** `make.format`, `make.lint_code`, `make.unit_tests`, `make.architecture` and `make.complexity` pass in the wave, and `make.static_analysis`, `make.unused_dependencies`, `make.tests_with_coverage` and `make.mutation_tests_diff` pass in the serialized verify step

**Depends on:** none

### Story 1.3: The events subscription field and its stream link

No protected change. Implemented by: the rust-implementer agent.

Goal: `EventStreamSubscription::events(types, lastEventId)` checks its arguments, registers one hub subscriber through `Subscriptions::open`, and reports "opened" or "refused" through a per-request `StreamLink`. Once the transport releases the link, it yields one `Event` per delivered frame and hands each event id to the link. It reuses the window, the replay from Kafka, the cap, the readiness rule, the shutdown and the slow-client handling of bundle 4 unchanged.

**Scope:**

- `crates/notification/event-stream/application/src/graphql/subscription.rs`:
  - `StreamPhase` (`Pending`, `Opened`, `Refused(HubError)`, `Released`);
  - `StreamLink` over `Arc<LinkInner { last_event_id: Option<HeaderValue>, phase: watch::Sender<StreamPhase>, delivered: AtomicU64 }>`, with the public `new`, `phase`, `decided`, `release` and `take_delivered`, and the crate-private `last_event_id`, `opened`, `refused`, `released` and `delivered` (`Ordering::Relaxed`);
  - the wiring error `MissingLink`;
  - `EventStreamSubscription` with the `#[Subscription]` field `events`, following resolver steps 1 to 4 of `architecture.md`;
  - the constant `TRANSPORT = "graphql-sse"`.
- `crates/notification/event-stream/application/src/graphql/subscription/tests.rs`: the test schema `Schema<EventStreamQuery, EmptyMutation, EventStreamSubscription>` on the test node, with the hub, the window, `Subscriptions` and `StaticTopic`.
- `crates/notification/event-stream/application/src/http.rs`: `#[cfg(test)] mod test_node;` becomes `#[cfg(test)] pub(crate) mod test_node;`, so that the `graphql` module's tests can import it (its items are already `pub(crate)`); nothing else in the file changes.
- `crates/notification/event-stream/application/src/graphql.rs`: the re-exports `EventStreamSubscription`, `StreamLink` and `StreamPhase` next to `EventObject`; `state(ctx)` used from the submodule.
- `crates/notification/event-stream/application/src/http/dto.rs`: `CursorFault::violation` and its tests.
- No manifest change; the crate already depends on `async-graphql`, `futures-util`, `tokio` and `serde`.

**Covers:** FR-1 (the field), FR-9 (the phases the transport reads), FR-10, FR-11, FR-12, FR-13, FR-16 (a refusal is reported, not logged), FR-18, FR-19 (a) and (c), FR-22, FR-23 (its tests), NFR-1, NFR-6, NFR-8 (100 types, the 64-bit cursor), NFR-9 (`replay failed` with `graphql-sse`, no new message), NFR-13 (placement), NFR-14.

**Tests:** the list "Event-stream application crate" of the architecture's "Test Strategy", run with `execute_stream` and a `StreamLink` in the request data, and unit tests of `StreamLink` and `CursorFault::violation`.

**Acceptance Criteria:**

- **Given** a started test node and a request with a `StreamLink` in its data, **When** `subscription { events(types: ["a"]) { id type data } }` runs through `execute_stream`, **Then** exactly one hub subscriber is registered (`subscribers` 1), the phase turns from `Pending` to `Opened`, and `decided()` completes
- **Given** that subscription before `release`, **When** the response stream is polled under paused time within a time-out, **Then** it yields nothing and the subscription takes no step: the scripted replay records no call and no window page is taken, also with a cursor below the window
- **Given** `release` was called, **When** live events of `a` and `b` are published, and cursors resume from the window and from Kafka (`StaticTopic`), **Then** each event of `a` is yielded once as `{"events":{"id":..,"type":"a","data":<event object>}}`, in strictly increasing id order with no gap or duplicate between the phases, and no event of `b` is yielded
- **Given** a subscription without a cursor that registered (`Opened`) and is not released, and events of `a` already in the window before registration, **When** further events of `a` are published before `release` and the stream is then released, **Then** exactly the events published after registration are yielded, in id order, without gap, and none retained before registration
- **Given** results yielded with `id` selected, aliased or not selected, **When** `take_delivered` is called after each result, **Then** it answers that result's event id, and a second call before the next result answers `None`
- **Given** each row of FR-13's table (`types: []`, an empty name, a name of 129 characters, `User.Created`, 101 distinct names, a malformed `Last-Event-ID` header, a malformed `lastEventId` argument without the header), **When** the field runs, **Then** the stream yields one error with the `message` `The request failed validation.`, `extensions.code` `VALIDATION_FAILED` and exactly the violation of that row, registers no subscriber, leaves the phase `Pending`, and the `LogRecorder` holds no entry
- **Given** faulty types and a faulty cursor together, several faulty names, or 100 distinct names with repeats, **When** the field runs, **Then** the types answer before the cursor, the first fault in list order answers, the 100 names are accepted, and no message holds a value of the request
- **Given** a valid header in the link and a malformed argument, **When** the field runs, **Then** the violation names `lastEventId` and no stream opens (a malformed argument is refused also beside a valid header); **given** a malformed header and a valid argument, **Then** the violation names `Last-Event-ID`
